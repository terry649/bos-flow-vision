# M4: controlled experiments, stress tests, and a label-integrity finding

M3 produced two RF-DETR Seg Medium models with similar test accuracy but different
behavior: the model trained on the hosted platform failed on blast fronts clipped by
the image boundary, and the locally trained model did not. M4 isolates the cause with
single-variable training arms, tests every model on held-out synthetic distribution
shifts, and adds a qualitative zero-shot test on published NASA AirBOS imagery.

All numbers are on synthetic data from the M1 generator. Every arm is a single
training run, so differences of about 0.01 in mAP are within run-to-run variation.
Full tables are generated by `scripts/m4_tables.py` into `tables.md`.

## Design

| Model | Training data | Training labels | Recipe | Serving |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M3 local | v1 (1,000 images) | exported locally | local (classification weight 1), 40 epochs | PyTorch |
| M3 hosted | v1 | as stored by the hosting platform | platform default | `inference` (ONNX) |
| A: hosted recipe | v1 | exported locally | platform default settings, run locally, 40 epochs | PyTorch |
| B: v2 data | v2 (v1 + 200 large, edge-clipped blast fronts) | exported locally | local, 40 epochs | PyTorch |
| C: vector encoding | v1, input channels (\|u\|, u_x, u_y) | exported locally | local, 40 epochs | PyTorch |
| D: platform labels | v2 images | as stored by the platform | local, 20 epochs | PyTorch |
| v2 hosted | v2 | as stored by the platform | platform default | `inference` (ONNX) |
| v2 hosted weights | v2 | as stored by the platform | platform default | PyTorch (downloaded weights) |

Every model is scored on the same 100-image test split, the same 20 blast-wave
sequences, and seven held-out stress sets generated from fresh seeds: a control set
from the training distribution; images built from two other displacement estimators
(Farneback, demons registration); camera noise and compression beyond the training
range; a longer background distance and wider aperture; Mach numbers and angles
beyond the training ranges; and composite scenes with two independent flows.

## Finding 1: the edge-clipping failure is a label-integrity problem

When a blast front crosses the image boundary, its mask can split into several
disconnected arcs. The export wrote such an instance as one COCO annotation with a
list of polygons, which the COCO format allows. The hosting platform stores one
polygon per instance, and after upload 107 of 108 multi-part annotations came back as
a single filled region (IoU with the true label as low as 0.007). All 1,050 single
polygons and all 237 ring labels with a hole (encoded with a zero-width bridge)
survived intact.

The failure follows the labels, not the recipe, the data volume, or the serving path:

| Model | Labels | Sequences with blast-energy error above 10% | Exponent (theory 0.4) |
|---|---|---:|---|
| M3 local | local | 0 of 20 | 0.3988 +- 0.0012 |
| A: hosted recipe | local | 0 of 20 | 0.3997 +- 0.0019 |
| B: v2 data | local | 0 of 20 | 0.3993 +- 0.0012 |
| C: vector encoding | local | 0 of 20 | 0.3987 +- 0.0012 |
| D: platform labels | platform | 2 of 20 | 0.3940 +- 0.0173 |
| v2 hosted | platform | 7 of 20 | 0.3705 +- 0.0418 |
| v2 hosted weights (PyTorch) | platform | 7 of 20 | 0.3731 +- 0.0405 |

- Arm A reproduces the platform's training settings locally and is clean, so the
  recipe is not the cause.
- Adding 200 edge-clipped fronts made the hosted model worse (5 to 7 failing
  sequences), because those are exactly the shapes whose labels were corrupted.
- The downloaded hosted weights fail identically in PyTorch, so the serving path is
  not the cause.
- Arm D trains locally on the same images with the platform-stored labels and
  reproduces the failure on 2 of 20 sequences (worst energy error 67%), both among
  the hosted model's failing sequences, with no hosted infrastructure involved. It
  fails less often than the hosted model, consistent with its 20-epoch budget, and it
  is the only locally trained model that fails at all.

**Fix.** A disconnected instance is now exported as one annotation per piece, each a
simple polygon; their union reproduces the mask. A round trip through the platform
confirmed that such polygons are stored exactly as sent. Two single-polygon
alternatives (bridging pieces with zero-width segments, and joining them with 1 px
corridors in the mask) enclosed spurious area and were rejected.

**Mitigation at inference time.** A thin-front gate (keep shock masks whose circle-fit
residual is below 5% of the radius) restores every model's blast analysis to an
exponent within 0.0018 of 0.4 and a worst energy error of at most 3.5%, and changes
at most 4 of 240 frames for models trained on correct labels.

## Finding 2: recipe, data, and encoding on the standard test split

| Model | Mask mAP50-95 | Shock AP50-95 | Wedge Mach error | Cone Mach error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M3 local | 0.826 | 0.573 | 0.2% | 1.9% |
| A: hosted recipe | 0.783 | 0.523 | 0.2% | 1.9% |
| B: v2 data | 0.822 | 0.573 | 0.2% | 1.6% |
| C: vector encoding | 0.817 | 0.551 | 0.2% | 2.1% |
| D: platform labels (20 epochs) | 0.800 | 0.534 | 0.2% | 1.6% |
| v2 hosted | 0.841 | 0.627 | 0.1% | 1.3% |

- Under a fixed 40-epoch budget, the platform's default settings (classification
  loss weight 5) train to lower mask quality than the local settings (weight 1).
- The extra edge-clipped data costs nothing on the standard split.
- Mach numbers recovered from predicted shock masks are insensitive to these
  differences: 0.1 to 0.2% for wedges and 1.3 to 2.1% for cones in every model.
- The hosted models score highest on the test split; the difference is concentrated
  in shock AP at strict IoU and does not carry over to the physics metrics.

## Finding 3: robustness to distribution shift

Mask mAP50-95 by stress set (full tables, including per-class values, in `tables.md`):

| Model | Control | Farneback | Demons | Harsh camera | Optics shift | Physics extrap. | Composite |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M3 local | 0.846 | 0.786 | 0.730 | 0.609 | 0.770 | 0.654 | 0.421 |
| A: hosted recipe | 0.814 | 0.764 | 0.681 | 0.610 | 0.701 | 0.605 | 0.429 |
| B: v2 data | 0.843 | 0.789 | 0.720 | 0.575 | 0.760 | 0.683 | 0.450 |
| C: vector encoding | 0.840 | 0.754 | 0.652 | 0.618 | 0.748 | 0.685 | 0.474 |
| D: platform labels | 0.840 | 0.784 | 0.692 | 0.570 | 0.767 | 0.672 | 0.434 |
| v2 hosted | 0.862 | 0.740 | 0.580 | 0.608 | 0.739 | 0.620 | 0.368 |

- **Estimator shift is the most consequential real-world shift.** Images built from
  demons registration instead of DIS cost 0.12 to 0.28 in mAP; the hosted v2 model
  and the vector-encoded model degrade most.
- **Input encoding is a trade-off.** Adding signed displacement components helps on
  composite scenes (best mAP 0.474 and best shock AP 0.148) and under harsh camera
  noise, and hurts under estimator shift, where other estimators produce noisier or
  differently biased components. Magnitude-only input is the safer default when the
  displacement pipeline may vary.
- **Composite scenes and harsh noise are the largest gaps for every model** (mAP 0.35
  to 0.47 and 0.52 to 0.62); shock AP on composite scenes is about 0.1 for all models.
  Multi-feature training scenes are the clearest next addition to the generator.

## Finding 4: qualitative zero-shot on NASA AirBOS imagery

On three published NASA AirBOS 4 images (grayscale renderings converted to a
magnitude proxy), the M3 local model outlines the outer shock envelope of a
supersonic T-38 (confidence 0.83) and of the lead aircraft in formation (0.48), and
misses nested, multiple, and curved interacting shocks. Those configurations do not
exist in the synthetic training data. See `nasa/`.

## Engineering notes

- Checkpoints trained from a platform export put the annotation group at output index
  0 and the classes in alphabetical order; locally trained checkpoints use training
  order. Serving now takes an explicit label order (`--labels`).
- Polygon mask responses cannot represent holes; serving requests RLE masks.
- ByteTrack confirms a track on its second match; first-frame detections are kept and
  attached to the track they precede.
- A soft GPU-cache limit (`PYTORCH_MPS_LOW_WATERMARK_RATIO=0.6`) cut the training
  footprint on Apple silicon from 31 GB to 20 to 25 GB without slowing training,
  which allowed evaluation to run in parallel.

## Limitations

- One training run per arm; no seed replicates.
- Arm D trained for 20 epochs (the question is qualitative); arm A was stopped at 40
  of a possible 100 epochs to match the other local arms.
- The M3 hosted model was evaluated on four of the seven stress sets.
- A hosted retrain on the corrected labels was not run.
- All quantitative results are synthetic; the NASA test is qualitative only.
